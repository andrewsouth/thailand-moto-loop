The Mae Hong Son Loop

Eight days on the ground in Chiang Mai, six of them on mid-size adventure bikes, at a pace for a group that keeps moving. Every stop from the tour page, with the rest days taken out.

Daylight rule: sunset is about 5:45 to 6:00 pm from November to February. Be off the mountain roads by then (fog, dogs, and unlit trucks). Days 4 and 6 only work with a 7:00 to 7:30 start.

Day by day

Times are targets for a group that rides steadily and keeps stops short. Distances come from OpenStreetMap routing. Each night shows a comfortable pick and a budget pick (rough prices per room from booking sites).

  1. Day 1

    Land in Chiang Mai

    no riding

    Fly Bangkok to Chiang Mai (CNX), about 1 hr 10 min; the Old City is 15 min by Grab. Wat Chedi Luang and Wat Phra Singh, then Tha Phae Gate and the Night Bazaar. Stop at the rental shop to sign papers and fit helmets.

    Beds
    ComfortableThapae Boutique House, by Tha Phae Gate, ~1,500 to 2,500 ฿; has triple rooms. BudgetPha Thai House, ~$20 to $35, twin rooms.
  2. Day 2

    Doi Suthep and the Samoeng loop

    ~140 km · 4 to 5 hr

    8:00 bikes. 8:30 Wat Phra That Doi Suthep before the crowds. 10:30 Samoeng loop through Mon Cham, lunch in Samoeng, back through Hang Dong by mid-afternoon. Sort out luggage, intercoms and riding order today.

    Roads
    Rte 1004 to Doi Suthep; 1096 out through Mae Rim; 1269 back through Hang Dong.
    Fuel
    Collect bikes full. Stations at Mae Rim, Samoeng and Hang Dong. Fill up tonight for tomorrow.
    Beds
    Same Chiang Mai hotel.
  3. Day 3

    Chiang Mai to Pai

    ~135 km · 3.5 to 4 hr

    8:30 leave. The 762 curves of Route 1095. 11:00 coffee and the viewpoint at Huai Nam Dang (about 6 km off the highway). 2:00 into Pai: hot springs or the Land Split. 5:00 sunset at Pai Canyon, then the Walking Street.

    Roads
    Rte 107 north to Mae Malai, then left onto 1095.
    Fuel
    Top up at Mae Malai (km 36); stations are sparse after the turn. Fill in Pai tonight.
    Beds
    BudgetBreeze of Pai Guesthouse, 1 min from Walking Street, ~800 ฿, easy parking. ComfortablePai Village Boutique Resort, from ~1,750 ฿ (parking limited).
  4. Day 4

    Pai to Mae Hong Son, by Tham Lod and Ban Rak Thai

    ~185 km · 5.5 hr

    7:30 leave, over the Kiew Lom viewpoint. 9:00 Tham Lod Cave: lantern guide and bamboo raft (about 2 hr). 11:30 lunch in Soppong. 2:00 Ban Rak Thai for Yunnanese tea on the lake. 3:30 Pang Ung reservoir. 4:45 into Mae Hong Son; sunset at Wat Phra That Doi Kong Mu, dinner at the Jong Kham Lake night market.

    Roads
    1095 west to Soppong and the cave spur; west to Na Pa Paek, north to Ban Rak Thai, down past Pang Ung to Mae Hong Son. The steepest, twistiest riding of the trip.
    Fuel
    Leave Pai full; top up in Soppong (small station). Then no confirmed station for ~125 km until Mae Hong Son, only village bottle fuel. A CB500X has about 380 km of range, so this is fine starting full.
    Beds
    ComfortableImperial Mae Hong Son Resort, ~1,550 ฿, large grounds. BudgetPiya Guesthouse, 200 m from Jong Kham Lake, pool, ~930 ฿.
  5. Day 5

    Mae Hong Son to Khun Yuam and Doi Mae U-Kho

    ~145 km · 4 hr

    7:30 Su Tong Pae bamboo bridge across the rice fields (12 km north). 9:30 south on Route 108. 11:00 Khun Yuam: the Thai-Japan Friendship Memorial Hall (WWII), lunch. 2:30 up to Doi Mae U-Kho for the sunflower hills (mid-November to early December) in the late light. 5:15 start back down the 27 km to town before dark.

    Fuel
    Fill in Mae Hong Son. Station in Khun Yuam; fill tonight, because tomorrow starts on the emptiest part of the loop.
    Beds
    BudgetYoont Hotel, rooftop view, owner gives road advice, from ~700 ฿. ComfortableKhunyuam Resort, mountain views, ~$30 to $50. Book ahead in sunflower season.
  6. Day 6

    Khun Yuam to Doi Inthanon, by Mae Sariang and Ob Luang

    ~260 km · 6.5 to 7 hr

    The long day. 7:00 leave. 9:30 Mae Sariang: fuel and coffee by the Yuam River. 12:00 Ob Luang gorge and its suspension bridge; lunch nearby. 1:30 through Hot and Chom Thong. 3:00 up Route 1009 into Doi Inthanon National Park, to Mae Klang Luang for the rice terraces in the late light.

    Fuel
    Leave Khun Yuam full. Fill in Mae Sariang (stations there capped sales during the March 2026 shortage, so don't arrive on reserve), again at Hot, and top off at Chom Thong before the climb (no stations in the park).
    Beds
    BudgetBan Kham Kee Homestay, Mae Klang Luang, breakfast and a firepit, ~600 to 1,200 ฿. ComfortableMea Klang Luang Villa, opened 2024, breakfast, ~2,400 ฿. It drops near freezing at night in Dec and Jan.
  7. Day 7

    Summit and back to Chiang Mai

    ~130 km · 3 hr

    5:45 ride up for sunrise at the 2,565 m summit and the Kew Mae Pan ridge trail (guide required; open about November to May). 9:00 the twin royal chedis. 10:30 Wachirathan Falls on the way down. 1:30 bikes back in Chiang Mai. Afternoon: Warorot Market, a massage, and khao soi.

    Fuel
    Most shops want the bikes back full. Fill up at the last station before the shop.
    Beds
    Back to the Chiang Mai hotel. Or fly out this evening if the flight times work, and save a night.
  8. Day 8

    Fly out

    no riding

    Fly Chiang Mai to Bangkok and connect home.

Before we go

  • Licenses. Each rider needs a motorcycle endorsement on their US license and an International Driving Permit that lists motorcycles (AAA issues them). Without both, police fines run 400 to 2,000 ฿, and travel and rental insurance usually won't pay out after a crash.
  • Rental shops. Get quotes from three shops: Mr Mechanic (Moon Muang Rd; 1,200 to 2,000 ฿/day; cash deposit and a passport copy, not the original), POP Bigbike (53 Kotchasarn Rd; panniers and top cases), and C&P Big Bike (next to POP, from 1,200 ฿/day). Ask about the crash excess (often 10,000 to 30,000 ฿) and whether third-party cover is included. Tony's Big Bikes may have closed.
  • Helmets. Required for riders and passengers; the fine went up to 2,000 ฿ in 2025. Bring your own or pay ~100 ฿/day for a better rental helmet.
  • When to go. November to February is cool and dry. Late November to early December catches the sunflowers, but avoid Yi Peng (around Nov 24 and 25, when Chiang Mai prices double or triple) and New Year week (Pai and Inthanon sell out). Mid-January to February is quieter and cheaper.
  • Fuel habit. Fill up in every town, even at half a tank. Stations along the loop ran dry in March 2026.
  • Spare time. The fast pace leaves little cushion. Carry a tire plug kit and a compressor, and agree ahead of time which stop gets dropped if a day runs late (Su Tong Pae on Day 5 and the Mae Sariang coffee stop on Day 6 are the easiest).
  • Gear. Warm layers for 5:45 am starts (near freezing on Inthanon in Dec and Jan), rain shell, disc locks (no hotel on the list promises secure parking, so ask for a gated or covered spot), intercoms, and an offline map.
  • Park fees. Foreigners pay 300 ฿ each at Doi Inthanon and Huai Nam Dang, 100 to 200 ฿ at Ob Luang, and 20 ฿ per motorcycle. Bring cash.
